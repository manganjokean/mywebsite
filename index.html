My Cybersecurity Awareness

Connecting my personal website and perspective to our IT Computer Security course.

Question 1

🧠 What does cybersecurity mean to you?

To me, cybersecurity means the practice of protecting digital devices, networks, and confidential information from unauthorized access, disruption, or destruction. It is about upholding the CIA Triad—ensuring that information remains Confidential, accurate and Integrated, and Available to those who need it, through both technical measures and proactive human awareness.

Question 2

🌍 Why is cybersecurity important?

Cybersecurity is critically important because virtually every modern system—from hospitals and power grids to banking systems and university portals—relies on computers and the internet. A breach or system shutdown can cause catastrophic financial losses, expose sensitive private records, and jeopardize public safety.

Question 3

🔑 What is one cybersecurity habit you currently practice?

One habit I strictly practice every day is enforcing Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) across all my accounts and using a password manager to generate unique, complex passphrases (over 16 characters). I never reuse passwords across services, and I use mobile authenticator apps rather than SMS to prevent SIM-swapping attacks.

Question 4

🎓 What cybersecurity topic do you want to learn this semester?

This semester in IT Computer Security under Prof. Xiwang Guo, I am most excited to learn about Network Defense & Vulnerability Assessment. I want to understand how firewalls, intrusion detection systems, and network segmentation stop attackers, and how to perform proactive security audits to patch vulnerabilities before they are exploited.

Assignment 1 – Cybersecurity Problem & Website Proposal

Students identify a real-world cybersecurity problem and propose a web application that addresses or demonstrates the problem.

Project Proposal

Proposed Web Application: “PhishShield Academy”

Author: John Manganelli (Senior) • Course: IT Computer Security • Instructor: Prof. Xiwang Guo

⚠️ Real-World Problem: Phishing & Credential Theft

  • The Threat: Social engineering causes over 80% of enterprise & university data breaches.
  • The Gap: Traditional training uses passive slides/videos that fail to build practical detection skills.
  • The Consequence: Users fall for spoofed domains, urgent admin alerts, and fake login portals.

💻 Deliverable 1: Website Concept

  • Application: PhishShield Academy — interactive training sandbox.
  • Core Mechanism: Hands-on dissection of simulated phishing emails & URLs.
  • Key Value: Immediate feedback demonstrating how attacks work safely.

👥 Deliverable 2: Target Users

  • University Students: Targets of fake financial aid & campus jobs.
  • Faculty & Staff: Handlers of sensitive administrative & student records.
  • IT/Security Learners: Students studying attack mechanics & defenses.

⚙️ Deliverable 3: Functional Requirements

  • Interactive Email Sandbox: Hover link inspection & header analysis.
  • URL Decoder Tool: Breaks down domain spoofing & lookalike characters.
  • Scenario Quizzes: Instant scoring with red-flag explanations.
  • MFA Defense Demo: Visual demonstration of MFA neutralizing stolen logins.

🛡️ Deliverable 4: Security Requirements

  • Zero Credential Retention: Never collects, logs, or stores real passwords.
  • Input Sanitization: Full mitigation against XSS and injection.
  • Encrypted Transport: Enforced HTTPS/TLS 1.3 across all pages.
  • Client-Side Isolation: Simulated exercises never contact real external targets.

🧟‍♂️ Deliverable 5: Potential Threats & Mitigations

Threat 1 Cross-Site Scripting (XSS): Malicious script injection via quiz inputs → Mitigated with strict HTML encoding.
Threat 2 Simulation Confusion: Users mistaking educational tests for real attacks → Mitigated with clear training watermarks.
Threat 3 Denial of Service (DoS): Automated bot request floods → Mitigated via client-side processing & rate limiting.

❓ Deliverable 6: Research Question

“Does interactive web simulation improve a student's ability to identify spear-phishing attacks significantly better than passive training videos?”

🎯 Deliverable 7: Project Objectives

  • Build Accessible UI: Responsive, modern frontend using HTML/CSS/JS.
  • Deploy 3 Scenarios: Financial aid scam, password reset, fake invoice.
  • Track Detection Rate: Measure accuracy gains via interactive tests.
  • Verify Secure Architecture: Enforce zero-data-retention standards.
